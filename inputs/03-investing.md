Value investing requires seeing what others miss. When the market panics, prices diverge from intrinsic value. The discipline is buying when everyone is selling — which means being comfortable with the possibility of being wrong for a long time before being right.

Portfolio diversification isn't about spreading risk — it's about ensuring you survive long enough for your thesis to play out. Concentration builds wealth; diversification preserves it. The tension between these two drives every allocation decision.

Compounding is the only force that turns small advantages into dominant positions. A 1% daily improvement compounds to 37x over a year. But compounding works in reverse too: small leaks in a system, left unchecked, compound into catastrophic failure. The asymmetry of compounding rewards early detection of both opportunity and decay.
