Donella Meadows identified leverage points — places in a system where small changes produce large effects. The highest-leverage intervention isn't adjusting parameters (tax rates, quotas) but changing the system's goals or paradigms. A thermostat set to 68F will fight any intervention that doesn't change the setpoint. Most policy debates adjust parameters while the goals remain unchallenged, which is why the system stubbornly returns to its previous behavior.

Stocks and flows reveal dynamics that snapshot thinking misses. A bathtub's water level (stock) depends on both the faucet (inflow) and the drain (outflow). Even if you reduce inflow, the stock continues rising until inflow equals outflow. Atmospheric CO2 works the same way — emissions reductions don't immediately lower concentrations. The delay between action and visible effect is where political will dies.

System archetypes recur across domains. "Fixes that backfire" appears in antibiotic resistance (short-term cure creates long-term superbug), addiction (substance relieves symptoms while worsening the underlying condition), and technical debt (quick fix creates future maintenance burden). Recognizing the archetype lets you skip the domain-specific analysis and go straight to the structural intervention.
