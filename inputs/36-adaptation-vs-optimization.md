Optimization finds the best solution for a known problem. Adaptation finds good-enough solutions for problems that keep changing. Evolution doesn't produce optimal organisms — it produces organisms that are fit enough to reproduce in their current environment. When the environment shifts, yesterday's optimal design becomes a liability. The cheetah's speed is useless when the savanna becomes forest. Optimization assumes a stable fitness landscape; adaptation assumes the landscape itself is moving.

Machine learning faces this tension directly. A neural network trained to minimize loss on a fixed dataset is optimizing. But if the data distribution shifts after deployment — concept drift — the optimized model degrades silently. Continuous learning, ensemble methods, and online algorithms sacrifice peak performance on the training distribution for robustness across distributions. The practical question isn't "how accurate?" but "how accurate under what range of conditions?"

Organizations that over-optimize become fragile. Toyota's just-in-time manufacturing eliminated waste by reducing inventory buffers to near zero. When the 2011 Tohoku earthquake disrupted supply chains, the optimized system had no slack to absorb the shock. Adaptive organizations maintain deliberate inefficiency — spare capacity, redundant suppliers, cross-trained employees — that looks wasteful until the environment shifts and the slack becomes survival.
