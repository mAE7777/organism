The best teachers don't transmit knowledge — they create conditions for discovery. Socratic questioning doesn't provide answers; it creates productive confusion that forces the student to construct understanding from their own mental models.

Scaffolding in education means providing support that gradually withdraws. A teacher builds temporary structures — examples, analogies, guided exercises — then removes them as the student develops internal competence. The scaffold is designed to become unnecessary.

Desirable difficulty: making learning harder in the short term improves retention in the long term. Spacing practice, interleaving topics, testing before teaching — these feel inefficient but produce deeper encoding. The paradox is that the conditions that feel like learning (fluency, ease) are often the conditions that produce forgetting.
