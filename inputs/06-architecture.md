Christopher Alexander's pattern language treats buildings as living systems. Each pattern — a courtyard, a window seat, a transition space between inside and outside — solves a human problem at a specific scale. Patterns compose: smaller patterns nest inside larger ones, creating coherent wholes from independent pieces.

Load-bearing walls serve double duty: they define space and carry structural load. When you remove a load-bearing wall, you don't just lose a room boundary — you threaten the entire structure above it. The most critical elements are the ones doing invisible work.

The best buildings age well because they separate layers that change at different rates. The site lasts centuries, the structure decades, the skin and services years, the interior layout months. Stewart Brand called this "shearing layers." Systems that mix fast-changing and slow-changing elements in the same layer become impossible to maintain.
