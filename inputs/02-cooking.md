Great sauces are built in layers. A French mother sauce starts with a roux — flour and fat cooked together — then builds: stock for depth, aromatics for complexity, reduction for concentration. Each layer transforms the whole, not just adds to it.

Fermentation is patience made tangible. Kimchi, sourdough, miso — they all require surrendering control to time and microbial action. The cook sets conditions but cannot force the outcome. The best fermented foods come from environments that are stable but not sterile: controlled chaos.

The difference between a good cook and a great one isn't technique — it's knowing when to stop. Over-seasoning, over-cooking, over-complicating. The restraint to leave something alone at the right moment is the hardest skill to develop.
