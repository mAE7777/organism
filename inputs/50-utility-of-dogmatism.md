Open-mindedness is overrated as a cognitive default. Bayesian reasoning requires strong priors to generate useful predictions — a "prior" of 50/50 on every proposition is not open-minded; it's uninformative. An experienced surgeon who dogmatically insists on sterile technique over a resident's shortcut is deploying justified rigidity. Dogmatism about well-established principles prevents reinventing failures that previous generations already suffered through. The trick is knowing which beliefs deserve protection from revision.

Institutional dogma — standardized procedures, checklists, protocols — prevents the catastrophic variance that individual judgment introduces. Aviation's insistence on following checklists regardless of pilot experience is dogmatic by design. The Checklist Manifesto documents how rigid adherence to surgical checklists reduced complications by 36%. Individual creativity is valuable, but in high-stakes domains, the floor matters more than the ceiling. Dogma raises the floor by constraining the worst-case behavior of the weakest practitioners.

Cultural dogma transmits hard-won survival knowledge across generations that can't verify it empirically. Kashrut dietary laws predate germ theory but effectively reduce foodborne illness risk in pre-refrigeration environments. Pacific Island navigation taboos encoded route knowledge in ritual form that persisted through centuries of oral tradition. The dogmatic transmission mechanism — "do this because we've always done it" — is epistemically crude but informationally robust. It preserves knowledge even when the rationale is lost, which matters more than elegance when the knowledge keeps people alive.
