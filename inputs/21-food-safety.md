HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points) transformed food safety by shifting focus from end-product testing to process control. Instead of inspecting finished products for contamination, you identify points in the production chain where hazards can be prevented, eliminated, or reduced to safe levels. A pasteurization step is a critical control point — if temperature drops below 72C for 15 seconds, the entire batch is suspect. Monitor the process, not just the output.

Cross-contamination follows invisible pathways. A cutting board used for raw chicken retains Salmonella in knife scars even after washing with soap. This is why professional kitchens color-code surfaces: red for raw meat, green for vegetables, blue for seafood. The system doesn't rely on memory or diligence — it makes the safe path the obvious path through environmental design.

Temperature abuse is the single greatest food safety risk. The danger zone (4-60C) is where bacteria double every 20 minutes. A pot of soup cooling on a counter for four hours can go from safe to containing millions of pathogenic organisms. Time-temperature control isn't one rule among many — it's the rule that contains most others.
