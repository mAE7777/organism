Jazz improvisation operates within constraints. A chord progression defines the harmonic space; rhythm section provides temporal structure. The soloist navigates within these boundaries, finding freedom precisely because the constraints are clear. Without structure, improvisation becomes noise.

Counterpoint — Bach's great innovation — weaves independent melodic lines that are each complete on their own yet create emergent harmony when combined. The voices don't blend; they maintain their identity while creating something greater than any single line. The tension between independence and coherence is the source of beauty.

Resonance in music isn't metaphorical — it's physical. Strike a tuning fork and nearby objects at the same frequency will vibrate in sympathy. The original signal doesn't push them; it creates conditions where their natural frequency is activated. Amplification through alignment, not through force.
