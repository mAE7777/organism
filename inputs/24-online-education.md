Completion rates for MOOCs hover around 5-15%, but this framing misunderstands the medium. Many learners audit selectively — watching specific modules relevant to their immediate problem without intending to finish. MIT OpenCourseWare's data shows that "drive-by learners" who engage briefly but purposefully outnumber completers ten to one. Measuring completion rates applies a classroom metric to a fundamentally different learning modality.

Spaced repetition systems like Anki exploit the forgetting curve discovered by Ebbinghaus in 1885. Reviewing material at increasing intervals — 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, 21 days — consolidates long-term memory with minimal total study time. The optimal review moment is just before the learner would forget, which algorithms can predict with surprising accuracy. The system fights the brain's natural tendency to discard unused information.

Peer assessment scales where instructor grading doesn't. Coursera's peer-graded assignments show that the median peer grade correlates at 0.85 with instructor grades when using well-designed rubrics. More importantly, the act of evaluating others' work deepens the reviewer's own understanding. Grading forces articulation of quality criteria that passive consumption never requires.
